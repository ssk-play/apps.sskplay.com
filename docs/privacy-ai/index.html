Privacy Policy — AI features

Effective date: 2026-09-04  ·  Last updated: 2026-09-08

This policy covers the apps published by sskplay (“we”, “our”) that send something you wrote to an AI model. Those apps keep records on servers we operate, which the rest of the catalogue does not, so they are not covered by our general privacy policy. Where the two differ, this page is the one that applies.

Which apps this covers

  • Review Diary — com.sskplay.a3diary (Android). A diary that writes a reflection on an entry when you ask for one.
  • endiary — com.sskplay.endiary (Android). An English-writing app that corrects your diary sentence by sentence.

Any app not named here follows the general policy instead. When we add an AI feature to another app, it is added to this list before it ships.

Most of what follows is true of both apps. Where they genuinely differ — and they differ on things that matter, starting with whether your writing is kept on our server at all — the text says which app it is talking about, and the table below is the short version.

Overview

Nothing goes to an AI model on its own. It goes when you ask for it — you request a reflection, or you finish a sentence and ask for it to be checked — and what travels is what that request needs.

Beyond that, each app keeps a small amount of data so it can work: an account or installation record, a usage counter, and anything you write to us through the feedback form. Review Diary additionally offers an encrypted backup and shows ads; endiary does neither. All of it is set out below.

What goes to the AI, and what does not

Both apps reach AI model providers the same way: through a gateway we run at a1.sskplay.com, which forwards the request to a provider. We use more than one provider, and which one handles a given request may change.

Your identity is never part of the request. Your name, email address, account or user ID, and device identifiers are not sent to the model. What is sent is the writing itself and the little that is needed to work on it:

  • Review Diary — when you request a reflection on an entry, that one entry is sent, with its mood value, date, and language. Your other entries stay where they are. Where a reflection refers to how you have been writing lately, what is sent is a style summary on the order of 30 characters, not your earlier entries.
  • endiary — when you finish a sentence, that sentence is sent together with up to 1,500 characters of the entry before it, so the correction makes sense in context, plus the language codes for what you are learning and what you want explanations in. When you ask for a final review, the whole entry and its title are sent.

When a request fails

If a request to a provider fails, our gateway writes an error log that can include part of the request and part of the response — up to 900 characters, kept for up to 90 days, then deleted.

This is not a hypothetical: some providers echo the content of a request back in their error response, so a fragment of what you wrote can end up in that log. The logs are used only to diagnose failures. They are not read for any other purpose, not linked to a name or email address, and not shared.

Model training

Text you send to the AI may be used to train or improve AI models. We route requests through providers whose terms permit this, so you should assume that what you send can be retained and used that way by the provider that handles it. We do not use it to train any model of our own.

Two things about the shape of that, because they are easy to get wrong in both directions.

It applies to what you send to the AI, and only that — the entry or sentence and the small amount that travels with it. Your account details are not part of the request in the first place, so what a provider may retain does not arrive attached to your name, email address, or user ID.

That is not the same as being safe, and we are not going to tell you it is. Text you write in a diary can be personal regardless of whether your name is on it, and once it has gone to a provider we cannot reach into it. The point of saying this plainly is so that you can decide what to write with the facts in front of you. If you would not want a piece of writing used this way, do not ask the AI to look at it — in both apps that choice is yours, request by request, and nothing is sent until you make it.

What we keep on our servers

Review Diary

Your diary text is not in our database. An entry passes through the gateway to get its reflection and is not kept afterwards, subject only to the error log above. What we do keep, on Cloudflare:

  • A random installation identifier, generated on your device. It is not the Android Advertising ID, not the Firebase app instance ID, and not derived from any device or account identifier — three different values with three different purposes. Reinstalling the app produces a new one.
  • Device model, OS version, app version, and language.
  • A per-installation monthly counter of how many reflections have been requested, to enforce the free limit. It counts requests; it does not record what they were about.
  • Feedback text you send from inside the app. Before it is stored, we automatically mask email addresses, phone numbers, national ID numbers, card numbers, and URLs.
  • Your subscription status and the purchase token that proves it.
  • If, and only if, you sign in to use backup: a one-way hash of your Google account email address. The address itself is not stored, and the hash cannot be turned back into it.

endiary

Your entries are stored on our server. That is not a side effect — it is the app: your diary is kept in your account so you can come back to it, and so a correction can refer to what you wrote before. They live in Google Cloud Firestore in the us-central1 region, under your account, and hold:

  • The title and body of each entry, its status, and when it was created and changed.
  • The final AI review of that entry — the score, the feedback, the polished version of your text, and the vocabulary it picked out.
  • A daily usage counter, to enforce the free limit. It counts; it holds nothing you wrote.
  • Feedback text you send from inside the app, together with your user ID, the category you chose, the screen you sent it from, the entry it refers to, the app version, the package name, and the platform. Feedback is stored exactly as you type it — nothing is masked — so please keep account numbers and the like out of it.

Accounts and sign-in

endiary signs you in with Firebase Authentication, either with your Google account or anonymously. An anonymous account needs nothing from you and still keeps your entries together; if it goes 30 days without being used, Firebase deletes it automatically, and the entries under it go with it. If you want your writing to outlive that, sign in with Google.

Review Diary has no account. It identifies an installation by a random identifier, and asks for a Google sign-in only if you turn on backup — see Encrypted backup below.

Encrypted backup — Review Diary only (optional, paid, off by default)

Backup exists so a diary survives a lost or replaced phone. It is off until you turn it on. endiary has no equivalent feature.

  • You choose a passphrase. A key is derived from it on your device (PBKDF2, 210,000 iterations) and the text of your entries is encrypted with it (AES-GCM) before anything is uploaded.
  • The key and the passphrase never leave your device. We cannot read the text of your entries, and neither can Google. What we hold for those is ciphertext.
  • Two fields are stored in the clear so the app can sort and display your entries without decrypting everything: the date and the mood value. The entry text is never in the clear.
  • If you forget the passphrase, nobody can recover the backup — not you, not us. There is no reset, because there is no copy of the key.
  • Turning backup off offers to delete the cloud copy.

Google sign-in is used for backup and nothing else here. It is what lets a restore on a new phone find the backup that belongs to you; without an account there is no way to identify a backup as yours after the old device is gone.

Crash reports and usage analytics — Review Diary only

Review Diary includes Firebase Crashlytics and Firebase Analytics, both from Google. endiary includes neither.

  • Crashlytics, when the app crashes: the stack trace, the device model, the OS and app version, and the state the app was in just before. Nothing is collected from debug builds.
  • Analytics: which screens you opened and which actions you took — saving an entry, requesting a reflection, opening the paywall, starting a purchase — along with an app instance ID assigned by Google and general signals such as device, region, and language.

Both are processed by Google under the Google Privacy Policy. You can turn either one off on its own, under ⋮ > Privacy in the app. They start on, your choice is remembered between launches, and switching either off costs you no features at all.

No diary content is attached to any of these events, and that is enforced in the code rather than left to care: titles, entry text, and reflection results are never passed as event parameters, and even the length of a piece of writing is reported as a bucket (short, medium, long) rather than an exact count, because an exact character count is itself information about what you wrote. A source check fails the build if an analytics call so much as references an entry or title variable.

One honest exception: a crash report can contain a fragment of what you typed, because an exception message can carry the input that caused it. We cannot rule that out, so we would rather say it than let you assume otherwise.

Advertising — Review Diary only

The free version of Review Diary shows banner ads provided by Google AdMob (Google LLC). Subscribing to premium removes them. endiary shows no ads. It contains no ad code, requests no ad, and shows you nothing.

One thing about endiary we would rather state than gloss over: the Google Mobile Ads library is still compiled into the build, and it brings with it the com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID permission and its own start-up component. The app never asks for an ad, but we are not in a position to promise you that the advertising identifier is therefore out of the library's reach.

Nothing you write is involved either way: no entry, and nothing derived from one, goes anywhere near an ad network. We intend to drop the unused library.

In Review Diary the ads and your diary are kept apart, and we want to be specific about what that means rather than leaving it to be inferred:

  • Ads appear on the list screen only. There is no ad on the screen where you write an entry, and none on the AI reflection screen.
  • The list shows each entry's title, or its first line when you did not give it a title, so a short excerpt of an entry can be visible on the same screen as an ad. That excerpt never leaves your device: it is not sent to the ad network, the ads SDK cannot read it, and it plays no part in which ad you see.
  • Your diary is not used to target ads. No entry, and nothing derived from one, is sent to an ad network.

What Google may use to choose an ad is its own signals, not ours: the device's advertising ID, approximate location derived from your IP address, and coarse device information such as model, OS version, and language. That is Google's processing, governed by the Google Privacy Policy and AdMob's ads personalisation policy. On Android you can reset or delete the advertising ID, and opt out of ad personalisation, in Settings > Privacy > Ads.

In the EEA, the UK, and Switzerland, Google's UMP consent form is shown first, and no ad is requested until you have answered it. You can reopen that form from inside the app at any time and change what you chose.

What we do not do

  • We never send your writing — or anything derived from it — to an ad network.
  • We do not build a profile of you from what you write, and we do not sell one or share one. What we do use is Google's own tooling in Review Diary, and we would rather name its reach than round it down: Firebase Analytics records what you do in the app against an app instance ID Google assigns; ad personalisation through the advertising ID does work across apps. Both are described above. endiary has neither.
  • We do not sell your data, and we do not share it with third parties beyond the service providers listed below, each of which only receives what it needs to do its job.
  • We do not read your writing for our own purposes. In Review Diary it is not in our database at all; in endiary it is stored so the app can give it back to you, not so we can read it. The places a fragment can end up somewhere we could open it are diagnostic and named above: the gateway error log after a failed request, and a crash report in Review Diary.

Service providers

  • Cloudflare — hosts the Review Diary server and its D1 database, and the a1.sskplay.com gateway used by both apps. Cloudflare Privacy Policy
  • Google — Google Play Billing for purchases; Firebase Authentication for sign-in; Cloud Firestore (us-central1) for endiary's entries and for Review Diary's encrypted backup; and, in Review Diary only, Firebase Crashlytics and Firebase Analytics. Google Privacy Policy
  • Google AdMob — serves the banner ads in Review Diary's free version. It receives the advertising signals described under Advertising, and no diary content. How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services
  • AI model providers, reached only through our gateway, and only for something you asked for. They receive the writing and the little that travels with it — never your identity — and may retain and use it as described under Model training.

In-app purchases

Where an app sells something, it is handled by Google Play Billing. Google receives and processes the payment — we never receive or store your payment details. Review Diary keeps the purchase token and whether the subscription is active, so the app knows what you are entitled to. endiary currently sells nothing.

Children's privacy

These apps are not directed to children under 13, and we do not knowingly collect personal information from them. If you believe a child has used one of them and sent us data, contact us and we will delete it.

Retention and deletion

  • Writing you send to the AI may be retained by the provider that handled it, as described under Model training. On our side the only trace is the gateway error log after a failed request: up to 900 characters, up to 90 days.
  • Review Diary entries stay on your device until you delete them, clear the app's data, or uninstall. They are not in our database.
  • endiary entries stay in your account until you delete them or ask us to delete the account. An anonymous account is deleted automatically after 30 days without use, and its entries go with it.
  • Encrypted backup is retained until you turn backup off (which offers to delete the cloud copy) or ask us to delete it.
  • Account or installation records, usage counters, feedback, and subscription status are retained while the app is in use, and deleted on request.
  • Payment data is retained by Google under its own policies, as are the advertising data AdMob collects and the crash reports and analytics events sent to Firebase.

How to ask us to delete your data

Email contact@sskplay.com. Tell us which app, and give us what identifies your records:

  • Review Diary — your installation ID, found by tapping the title on the diary screen seven times.
  • endiary — the email address you signed in with. For an anonymous account, write to us from the app's feedback form so we can match the request to your user ID; an anonymous account also disappears on its own after 30 days of not being used.

We respond within 30 days.

Your choices

  • Do not ask for a reflection or a correction, and nothing you wrote goes to an AI model — request by request, the choice is yours.
  • In endiary, delete an entry and it is removed from your account. Stop using an anonymous account and it is deleted after 30 days.
  • In Review Diary, leave backup off and no diary is uploaded. The installation record is sent either way, and feedback is sent only when you write some.
  • In Review Diary, turn crash reports or usage analytics off, individually, under ⋮ > Privacy in the app. Both start on, your choice is remembered, and turning either off takes nothing away.
  • Subscribe to Review Diary premium and the ads go away. In the EEA, the UK, and Switzerland you can also reopen Google's consent form from inside the app and change your advertising choices at any time.
  • Uninstall, and what was on the device goes with it. Server-side records are deleted on request as described above.
